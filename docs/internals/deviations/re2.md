# RE2 deviations

RE2 play is being ported to exactness (Phase C of #244), checked tick by tick against the original with `scripts/oracle-re2.py` (`oracle/re2.md`). Every place where RE2 play knowingly still differs from the original is listed here, so that the rest of the port has the list to work from.

## Player

The pad's walking, running, backing, turning, hurt clips and scripted motions (`player2`, a port of `Player_move`), collision and `Sce_Damage` areas match the original on random walks around room 100 for both characters. On the route, random walks through rooms 101, 11A and 11B, each entered through its door, match too (`walk_101.txt`, `walk_11a.txt` and `walk_11b.txt`, `oracle/re2.md`): `Rnd`, the player, `mot`, the camera, Kendo and every zombie on every tick, with the zombies on and with them dead, through the zombies' grabs and, in room 11B, to the end of the game over. The back walk (`pl_mv03`, `0x4da7c0`) looks at an enemy: unhurt, every 15 ticks, it takes clip 1 while `FUN_004daa60` finds one in play within 5000 units, alive, upright, within 800 of straight ahead, in clear sight head to head and facing the player, and clip 0 when none is, keeping the frame (clip 0's on a multiple of 7). The port backed on clip 0 only, so these walks used to part from the original on the first tick of backing up with a zombie in view. Kendo's `Life` is -1, so he doesn't count. The search's `+0x1B4` (the enemy it found) isn't kept, and the back walk's two bytes at `+0x16A` and `+0x16B` are their own fields in the port, where the original shares them with other routines. The cut of a third of the step that the back walk, the walk and the run make when `Water` (`+0x10C`) is more than 500 below `+0x1C2` isn't ported. Those rooms have no water.

Running was measured on the whole route, each room entered through the previous room's real door (`run_118.txt` to `run_200.txt` and `run_102.txt`, `oracle/re2.md`). Each walk runs in every direction, with diagonals, backing, quick stops and an action press while running, for 2356 ticks after the door (4620 for the chain from room 100 to room 102). Through the doors 102 to 118, 119 to 11A, 11A to 11B, 11B to 103 and 103 to 200, with the run-heavy walk and with a plain random walk, and through 118 to 119 with the plain walk, `room`, `cam`, `p.x`, `p.y`, `p.z`, `p.angle`, `p.beh`, `p.as`, `p.hp` and `mot` match on every tick, including the zombies' hits in 11A and 11B (to the game over in 11B). The chain 100 to 101 to 102 (Kendo's scene, then room 102) matches the same fields for all 4620 ticks apart from `mot` until the first step at 1300, as `door_101_102.txt` does, and `rng` and the zombies match on every tick but the door's. Room 102's zombies used to part from the original at aligned tick 3920, slot 1 taking routine 10 (the wall a script chose) where the original stays in 1: arklay kept an enemy's interaction area (`+0x0A`) from one tick to the next, so room 102's script still saw the zombie in area 4 after it had left and raised `Sce_free0`'s 0x8000. The original clears every enemy's and object's areas to 0xFF after each scheduler pass (`Sce_work_clr_at`), and arklay now does too. Room 119's camera cut matches: the script's `cut_replace` swaps two camera ids in every zone's `cam_from` and `cam_to` (`Cut_replace`; the gates are not touched), so the original cuts from camera 1 to camera 4 at `x` 2254 on `z` -19080, on the same tick as arklay, and in `run_119.txt` every field matches from there through a zombie's hit from aligned tick 757 and the game over that follows. `cut_replace`'s last step, switching to camera `b` when camera `a` is the active one, isn't ported.

## Sound

- **The door's room load.** With a sound device, `Snd_bgm_ck` holds the old room's clear and the new room's load 90 ticks while the door's animation plays on, so the room still comes back on the tick it does without sound; this holds the same ticks (and `--trace --sound` traces as the oracle's `--sound` does). The 90 ticks are the original's 60 Hz door frames, about 1.5 s, the fade `Snd_bgm_ck` starts (`0x69346f` = 0x5a) when the next room's main differs. arklay paces a door's ticks at 16 ms in play and in `--sound-wav`, so the hold lasts as long. The music under it follows the original's frames: `Snd_se_call` counts the fade after `Set_door` in the same frame (across a stage change, from the next frame), stops the sequences when it reaches 1 and frees the banks on the frame after; the held `Set_room`'s `Snd_bgm_set` loads the next room's music on the clear frame and `Snd_bgm_play_ck` starts it on the load. On the route from room 100 to room 200 the traced `bgm` and `xavol` fields match the original's on every tick of every room, doors included. `Door_snd_trans`, which rewrites the table once seven of the item flags 0xF8 to 0xFF are set in a game with `fg_system`'s `0x1000000`, isn't ported: nothing sets that bit yet.
- **A music bank with no track.** `Snd_bgm_main` and `Snd_bgm_sub` load the id's `Common/Sound/BGM` bank (`MAINxx.BGM`, `SUB_xx.BGM`) and succeed whatever tracks its `bgm_lut` or `sbgm_lut` row names, so `Snd_bgm_set` gives the sequence its word's hold (`Seq_ctr[1]`); only a missing bank (or the word 0xFF) gives 0xFF, and then nothing loads, not even the row's tracks. Main 9 names no track but has a bank: room 201's licker scene writes `c449`, and its sequence holds 1, not 0xFF (`route_200_208`). The retail data has no bank for mains 0x26, 0x32, 0x3B, 0x3C and 0x3F, nor for subs 0x38 to 0x3D and 0x3F; arklay keeps that list (`bgm2::main_bank`, `sub_bank`) rather than reading the banks, which aren't packed, and no bank is over `Snd_bgm_main`'s size limit.
- **A door to a new sub.** When only the next room's sub differs, `Snd_bgm_ck` (0x4ECBE0) fades a sub sequence that is in state 1 only while its channel's DirectSound buffer plays (`0x4347B0(6, n)`'s bit 0), and gives it state 0x32. arklay takes a sub sequence with a track loaded as playing, so one with no track on its channel keeps its state: room 107's sub 5 has none on its second channel, and its state stays 1 through the door into room 108, as in the original (`leon_a_106_108`). A sub `sce_bgm_control` started without its loop (its op 3, and op 1 in the rooms `plays_once` lists) stops playing at its track's end, which arklay counts in ticks at 30 a second from the track's length (read from the pack, with or without an audio device), as it does a voice line's. `SsPlay` on a sub that plays already only turns its loop off where it is, so it ends at the end of that pass; the original's buffer ends by the clock, so a recording's pace moves it. No recorded run has a sub play to its end.
- **The music's mix.** Tracks keep their channels, with the script's pan (`SetPan`'s `pan - 1`, 23 mB a step) and volume on top, as `SetPan` and `SetVolume` do; `SetPan`'s forced centre for 11025 Hz and config-flagged tracks, and room 115's -15 in `SetVolume`, aren't ported. Against the original's capture over the route (`--sound-wav`, `oracle/re2.md`), per-tick levels agree within 0.5 dB on both channels in rooms 101, 102, 11A, 11B and 200, and the channels correlate as the original's do. Room 100's right channel is 3.4 dB louder than the original's (measured): its pan and volume laws are the original's, and solo renders put the difference on `main00`'s two tracks, but its source wasn't found. Rooms 118, 119 and 103 can't be compared on the music: their traces start from a warp, and the original's music had started before the warp's room was entered (assumed from the right channel matching about 130 ticks later).
- **Sound effects.** `Snd_se_on`'s room-specific rules and loops aren't ported. `se_on` from an object's work uses the player's position. A tick's sounds are placed from the camera cut the tick started on. On the door runs with `--sound` from room 100 to room 200 (`door_101_102.txt` and the other doors, `oracle/re2.md`), the traced `snd` field (every sound started, with its type, slot, volume and pan) matches the original's on every tick, the zombies' sounds in rooms 118, 119 and 103 included, so `Snd_se_on`'s rules aren't needed there. Not ported: `Swap_Cbuff`'s reload in stage 5 room 10's cut 0 (`fg_status` 0x80000); `fg_system`'s 0x200, which keeps the load pending across ticks; the other `Movie_set` callers (`St_exit`, `Ex_door`, the pause), whose holds arklay does not take from this counter (`movie_on`'s two frames and a film's last black one go through it); and `WinMain`'s frameskip, which leaves a frame unpresented without counting the hold down. From a warp into room 200, every frame from tick 14 to 375 is within a mean error of 0.25 of the original's (0.19 over all 376). Ticks 0 to 13 differ (6.5 down to 0.8): the original is black on ticks 0 and 1 and has its letterbox bars fully in by its first frame, because its scene started during the warp, while arklay's `--room` start fades in and slides the bars in.
- **Models at the near plane.** The original's `Marni::TransObject` gives each corner of a model's triangle a depth of `1 - (H/2)/z`, H the cut's projection distance (`WinMain` sends the 0x3e00 matrix prim each frame with `DWORD_0067ca04 = Global_prj`), and the device clips each triangle where that depth reaches 0, at view z = H/2, keeping a corner on it. The OT key on `TransZ` is clamped, not culled. arklay used to drop a whole triangle with any corner nearer than twice the projection distance, so a model right under the camera lost most of its body. RE 2's models are now clipped at H/2 instead, on both backends: the lit ones, and a door's (`Door_model_init` types its prims 0x158 as `MarniSortEmdGallery` does, and `Marni_door_disp0` goes through `AddPrimitiveScaler` to the same `TransObject`). The software rasteriser cuts each triangle along the plane (`clip_triangle_near`), and the GPU's mesh shader writes the plane as a clip distance with the device's depth clip off. A triangle with a corner behind the eye is culled by its facing in view space, since its screen winding no longer says. These triangles' pixels take the depth of their own view Z, perspective-correct, in both backends: the corners a clip makes are exact that way, and it is the original's order too, since `sz` is affine in 1/z, which is linear on screen. Every other model keeps the reference's depth, linear on screen. Over the 109 runs with frames, no window got worse and two got better: `leon_a_113_10a`'s 360 to 501 (a zombie lying under the camera on 453 to 457) from 1.26 to 0.87, tick 455 from 7.91 to 2.22, and `claire_a_207`'s 650 to 1579 from 1.49 to 1.06. What's left at 455 is the texture's filtering: the original's looks bilinear there. Recorded doors do come that close: on `leon_a_204_entry`'s door, ticks 796 to 801, a panel at the frame's left edge is inside `2 * H` and was dropped, and it is now drawn as the original draws it (tick 796 from 1.49 to 0.35). Seven runs through doors gain about 0.01 on their whole-run mean for it, and no window moves. The view-Z depth moves `boxes_306`'s mean by +0.0009 (at most 0.003 a frame), and `leon_a_204_entry`'s 766 to 776 by +0.004. The GPU path hasn't been rendered on a device here.
- **Script fades.** `sce_fade_set` and `sce_fade_adjust` run all four fades, over the whole screen and only in black or adding modes. Mode 3, used once, adds, and the colour mask, which the campaign always leaves at 7, is ignored. `Sce_fade_adjust`'s hold in stage 6's lit rooms isn't ported.
- **Attached effects.** `Esp_call` keeps the matrix it was given (`+0x4c`), a pointer to it (`+0x6c`) and the vector on it as the slot's base (`+0x2c`), and `Esp_pos` places the slot at the matrix's turn of the base plus its place, with the accumulator turned by the slot's direction. While a slot has flag 0x0800 `Esp_pos` copies the matrix from the pointer again on each tick, so the slot follows it. `Game_loop` draws every body (`Joint_trans2`, `ko_joint_trans2`) before `Esp_move`, so a slot on a body's part follows the part's `Workm` as this tick's draw leaves it, and a call copies it as the last draw left it. arklay models both: an effect on a part keeps the part (the player's or an enemy slot's) and the vector, and each `Esp_move` takes the parts' matrices that tick's draw leaves (`Effects::anchors`). For the player those are the parts' `Workm` as the move left them (on a stopped tick, which runs no move, the held pose where the body is, as `Joint_trans2` draws it then too), moved by the pushes after it (`Oba_ck_em2`, `Sca_ck_em`) and by a script's move before `Esp_move`; for an enemy, its pose at the end of its run with `Em10Dm00`'s and `FUN_00457e60`'s lean (part 0's `M` turned about z on its own side, part 1's by the transpose on its parent's; `FUN_00457e60`'s first tick falls into its next step with its blood's vector `(0, 300, 0)` still in `local_48`, so that tick's lean also turns 300 about y), a licker's parts from `J_seq2`'s matrices (below), its parts off the skeleton (`Parts_down`'s after their fall, a pulled arm, a kicked head) composed from their own matrix, and a part that isn't drawn (`Be_flg` bit 0 clear) kept where the last draw left it. The effects on a part are the shot's flash, cases and smoke (`pl_w02_t2_move`, `pl_w03_t2_move`, `FUN_004d6150`'s flash), the player's blood when held or dragged, the footsteps' ripples, and every `Esp_call` an enemy makes at a part: its blood and flames (`effect` in its script, `Em10Dm01`'s on its speed `&Spd_x`), and the blood of a part down as it bounces (`Parts_down`), of a kicked head and of a pulled arm's first tick, each on that part's own matrix. arklay names the part by the enemy's slot, where the original's pointer is into the enemy's work: a slot's next occupant with a following effect still out would move it only if the original reuses the same work's parts for it, which isn't checked (no recorded run has an enemy replaced under a following effect). The splats and puffs a slot leaves (`Esp_29`, `Esp_2A`, `Esp_0C`) are `Esp_call3`s on its own pointer and base, so they start on the same part. A room object's twinkle (`Sce_kirakira_set`, `Esp_call` on the object's matrix `+0x24`) follows the object as a script or a push moves it and goes when `Esp_kill` names that matrix (`St_get_item`); its turn of the base is not modelled, so a twinkle with a non-zero base on a turning matrix is placed as if the matrix did not turn. `Esp_kill` on a part's matrix (`FUN_004b9260`, the kicked head's blood) isn't modelled. Four things the blood and drool needed to start where the original's do: `Rot_neck_em`'s `Neck_Dir_ZY` aims from the neck's `Workm.t` (`+0x5C`) as the last draw left it, not from the tick's pose, which turned a stomped crawler's hidden head 14 to 23 units of angle off in `leon_a_106_108`; `FUN_00453e40`'s step 6, the start of a get-up, clears `+0x21B`'s 0x02 (the head held still) so the head follows the player again as the zombie rises, and with `Timer3` 1 bleeds once more (`Esp_call(8000)` on part 0); a licker's `Em22_before` starts its drool on part 9 as the last draw left it; and the licker plays its clips through `Mot_play2`'s `Joint_move2` and `J_seq2`, which, unlike the zombies' and the player's `J_seq`, eases each part's matrix rather than its angles: in a blend it mixes the last matrix with `RotMatrix` of the frame's angles (`HMatrix`, `0xFFF - late * Hokan` of the new) and makes rows 0 and 1 unit again (`NormalMatrix_0`), leaving row 2 as it is, and with no blend left it takes `RotMatrix` with rows 0 and 2 made unit (`NormalMatrix_2`). Eased angles put a licker's mouth up to 70 units off over a long blend. The draw composes the same matrices. `Em_24`'s init (`FUN_0046cfa0`) poses through `Joint_move`, plain `J_seq`, where `Em22_Init` uses `Joint_move2`, so a 0x24 licker's `J_seq2` starts from the raw `RotMatrix` of its init pose. The pool's `esp` runs (`oracle/re2-regress.d/esp.toml`), `esp_spider_shots_302`, `esp_water_walk_302`, `esp_leon_a_106_108`, `esp_street_shoot` and `esp_licker24_fight`, match the original's on every tick, and so do which effects are out and how many (`espk`, `espn`) in all of them. The shell case's place also sets its bounce's pan (`Snd_se_3D`): with the gun's matrix the last draw left, after the push, `leon_a_106_108`'s case at 2064 pans as the original's.
- **The sheets.** A room's effects use the low-resolution TIMs in its RDT, not `espdat1` and `espdat2`, and the shared ones `espcore.bin`'s six low-resolution TIMs (`Graphicsprdata == 0`), not `tex216.adt` and `tex316.adt`.
- **Collisions and pauses.** `fg_stop`'s 0x10000000 holds every kind but 0x15, 0x16, 0x1A, 0x1C and 0x28, as `Esp_move` does. `Game_loop` skips `Esp_move` on the ticks of play that don't reach the entity loop (`DWORD_00991fc0` is 0: `Die` running, the `0x8000000` path, and the tick a pause, status or map request is taken), and the passes of states 1, 3, 9 and 0xB keep the last tick's choice. arklay doesn't model the gate as such; through `street_shoot`'s game over the pool's count and kinds match the original's on every tick. The game over's clear is `Esp_die` (every kind but 0, and kind 5's rows 2 and 3); `Die_4B9350`, the clear of every slot that `Die_move_wait` takes when `fg_system & 4` is set, isn't modelled. `Esp_05` checks `Water_ck` and the ground under it (`Sca_ck_hit`): what falls below a water area's level goes there, in a splash (`Esp_call3` kind 0x1a, row 1, of its size), so a spent case in room 302 doesn't bounce.
- **Smoothing.** Effect quads aren't tweened at high frame rates.

## Game flow

- **A room's load and `fg_status`.** Every room load, a door's, a warp's, a new game's and a `--room` start's (and its setup script's re-run), clears `fg_status` as `Set_room` does (`& 0xFFF04000`, `0x4DE93E`), so a scene's bit, a voice line's bit, the quakes and the camera's lock don't outlive their room. arklay clears it at the start of the room's scripts' start (`run_re2_init`), where the original clears it at the load's start; none of `Set_room`'s later steps writes it. A `--room` start whose setup sets a flag that skips the room's entry scene (`weapon_pickup_200`'s 4:6) used to keep the scene bit its first start raised, and with it the letterbox bars, for the whole run. `Set_room`'s other clears on the same lines (`_fg_rbj_set` and two of the player's work bytes) aren't checked against arklay.
- **Debug jumps.** The player arrives through the door back from a neighbour room. With no such door the player stands in the room's own first door, and with no door at all under the first camera. The cut is the first of the door's, the zone's and the rest that shows the player. The original has no such jump. In rooms 200 and 500 no cut shows the placement.

- **Pick-ups.** `Sce_Item` opens the get-item window (`0x4FD560`), which holds the world from the next tick, as the original's game mode 2 does: the question shows on the seventh tick, a yes takes the item 20 ticks after the answer with "You've taken the ...", a no counts out 15 ticks, a full inventory says "You can't carry any more items.", and play comes back 11 ticks after the last message closes, with the player's motion restarted. The question types, takes its answer and plays its cues as the message code does ("Messages"), and the window plays the cancel cue when the last message has closed or a no has counted out (its sub 0, `0x4FD701`). The inventory, the item's flag, the model and the cues all change on the original's tick (`oracle/re2.md`, "Items"). The window draws the original's art (`0x4FD560`'s draw list) with the status screen's script in its `get` mode (`games/re2/ui/main_menu.lua`): the item page's frame, portrait, condition, trace, slots and counts with no tab lit and no cursor or name, CHECK's panel at the window's origin (7, 78) with the item's still picture from `itemdata.bin` at (23, 88) (`0x4FFF70`; the PC original shows a picture here, not a model), and, after a yes, the picture's copy flying under the still to the slot the item takes, right by 8 more than its steps left a tick (13 or 15 steps), by the slot's step down a tick, and 6 and 4 smaller a tick down to an icon's 45x30, as sub 2 moves it (`0x4FD7E6`, from the slot's start in `0x53DCA8`), drawn from sub 1's tick to the one before sub 3 (`src/game/item_get2.rs`). It opens and closes on the status screen's fades: the room darkens on the window's second and third ticks, three ticks of black, the screen fades in from the seventh (when the question shows) over four, and from the cancel cue it fades out as the status screen's close does, then the room fades back in. The trace steps on the ticks the screen is drawn, as the status screen's does. While the window is up the original's `Cut_no` is 0: its screen saves the cut and clears it on the open's sixth tick (`0x501CD9`) and puts it back on the close's eighth (`0x502027`), while the room isn't drawn. Only the trace's `cam` shows it, and arklay's trace does the same. An item whose action has bit 0 is picked up crouching: `Sce_Item` sets the player's routine to 1, 6, 0 instead of opening the window, after that tick's `Player_move` as the original checks its areas, and `pl_mv06` (`0x4DAFF0`) plays the PLD's clip 6 and opens the window on the tick after the clip ends (sub 2), with the player held in sub 3. `pl_br06` reads no keys. The window's close zeroes the routines and runs `Player_move`, so `Pl_init` stands the player up, with clip 2 at frame 1, after a crouch and a standing pick-up alike (`0x4C000D`). `pl_mv06`'s subs 4 to 6, which play the crouch backwards and then go to routine 1, 0, are ported, but nothing on the route reaches them. An area whose `Super` has bit 0x80 is relative to a body (`Sce_at_check`, `0x4E9150`): with bit 0x40 the player, the second player or an enemy slot (`Super & 0x3F` less 2), otherwise an object, and the area moves by that body's x and z each time it is checked. `aot_reset` writes only an area's first three data words, so an item keeps its model and action (`0x4E5600`), also through a reset to another kind and back: room 101's shotgun is put out (`aot_reset` to kind 0) as the room loads and made an item again, with four shells, on the second check of Kendo's body, and is still taken crouching. An item area that a script runs (`aot_on`) chooses its crouch in the scripts' pass, after `Player_move`, so the crouch's first frame plays on the next tick, as from a press. The window's close leaves `fg_stop` at `0xEF000000`, input bit included, for the next tick's `Pad_set`, as a door does: a direction held through the close moves the player a tick later. While the status screen is up, `Cut_no` is 0 on the same ticks as the window's, from the open's sixth tick to the close's eighth (`weapon_pickup_101`). Room 103's key is both: an area on zombie Brad (`Super` 0xC2) that only the enemies' pass checks (`sat` 0x32) until his death's `aot_reset` makes it the player's (0x31). The new game's choice changes the counts as the original does (`src/scd2/host.rs`, `sce_item_count` and `st_get_item_count`): `Sce_Item` (`0x4E96E4`) writes its count back into the area before the check, ARRANGE MODE's ink ribbons as 2 and then HARD's table (the handgun rounds' 15 as 10 and any other count as 20, the shells 5, the magnum rounds 6, the grenade, flame and acid rounds 4, the bolts 12), and `St_get_item`'s take (`0x4FD88A`) applies HARD's table to the same area again. The table isn't idempotent for the handgun rounds, so an area of 15 is 10 at the check and 20 at the take, and a refused one is 10 until the next check makes it 20. The take adds to the stack the check chose, with the original's byte add, so 240 rounds and HARD's 20 wrap to 4. The `item_take_hard`, `item_take_arrange_hard`, `item_merge_wrap_arrange_hard` and `weapon_pickup_200_hard` runs match the original on every tick (`oracle/re2.md`, "Items"). What's left:
  - The flying copy is a scaled quad that the original rasterises at 640x480, so its texels land differently from arklay's at 320x240: those frames differ by up to 1.45 in `item_take_102.txt`.
  - The refusal and the full inventory use the same art and fades, but no recorded run checks their frames.
  - `pl_mv06`'s sub 0 sets bits 0xC0 of `0x989ED0` and sub 6 clears them. arklay doesn't model that word, and the room 103 run doesn't show what the bits change.
  - The two-slot weapons (`0x502570`: ids 0x0E to 0x12), their message with one slot free and the slot shift that makes room for them aren't ported; none is on the route.
  - Items 0x4E and 0x52 (picked by `0x509CA0`) go to the personal slot in the original, sub-step 0x13; arklay puts them in a free slot.
  - The item flag goes to the stage 1 to 3 bank; the original uses another from stage 4 (`0x98EBD4`).

- **Messages.** The item names, the item descriptions and the messages the executable draws itself are its English tables, which the converter reads from `bio2 1.10.exe` (`src/convert_re2/exe.rs`): the tables Classic REbirth repoints the readers at (`0x52F1F8` for the names, `0x5324D8` for the system messages and descriptions, `0x52F9B4` for the door messages). The status screen and the get-item window pass `Mess_set` their own place, `0xAF0010`: the first line's ink at (16, 177), the item page's name on the same line, `Yes` at 100 and `No` at 150. A message in play is placed by `Mess_set` itself, whatever its caller passes: attr `0x100` (the executable's own messages: a door's, a desk's, the typewriter's, `Key_lost`'s and room 200's computer's question) and `0x300` (a room's, from `message_on` and `Sce_Message`) both put it at `0xB90022`, 18 right of and 10 below the status screen's, with a `{branch 0}`'s `Yes` at 210 and `No` at 260 (`choice_data_e`'s first entry). Attr `0x200` (`0xBA0022`, a description) has no caller. A room's text is its RDT's English list (`rdt + 0x40`), so a line with text types at that list's length. Classic REbirth changes the executable's read from `rdt + 0x3C` to `rdt + 0x40` (`0x4C8B7C` and `0x4C8C8B`), and the oracle runs that build. On the route, rooms 101, 103, 11A and 200 have both sections, and they differ: the `rdt + 0x3C` ones don't read as English in the Western table, so the text the executable shows in those rooms isn't checked. A voiced line has no text in the list, so its timing is the original's. The text types as `Mess_disp` (`0x4C8DCE`) types it. It shifts every delay left by one while `0x98F07A` is clear, which it is on the status screen and in the get-item window (the original's trace of `item_take_102.txt` has it 0 from the window's opening to its close, and 2 in play; the status screen's opening clears it at `0x501C37` and its close, `0x501EA0`, sets it back to 2 at `0x501FDC`, which in the original's `inv_check.txt` is tick 908, two ticks before play resumes, so a room message after the status screen is unshifted): there a glyph takes two ticks, the first on the tick after the request, and a page's or the end's count is doubled. In play the byte is 2 and nothing is shifted: a room's message and a door's type a glyph a tick from the request's, and the end counts its own ticks, so room 101's first voiced line (`{timed 20}`) holds `wsleep` for 20 ticks after its request's, as the original's message state (`0x98504C`) shows. Spaces and line breaks cost nothing either way. Every request turns the speed-up on, and while the confirm or cancel key is held the timer drops by four a tick and types two glyphs at once at a delay of two or three, one at a longer delay (`0x4C8F01` to `0x4C8F1C`; shifted, the delay is never below two, so the four-glyph arm runs only in play, at a delay of one). A yes/no ignores the confirm key for 16 ticks after its row shows (`+0x3BAA`, `8 << 1`; 8 in play), though the choice moves meanwhile; the answer plays the decide cue for yes, unless the argument after `0xFB` has `0x20`, and the cancel cue for no, for a system or status message (flag `0x2000` of the request) or a question whose argument has `0x60`, and a move always plays the cursor cue (`0x4C9593`). On the answer's tick the question still draws, without its choice row; a message closed by a press draws nothing on that tick. The converter keeps the argument as the `{branch}` tag's third byte. `xa_on` plays its line and `wsleep` waits for it, for as long as the line plays with sound, and for `Xa_control`'s count without (half that), so a trace matches the reference, which runs without sound. `message_on`'s last two bytes are a word of `fg_stop` bits, which the message holds in `fg_stop`'s top half while it shows and which its dismissal takes down again (`0xFFFF` on a check in play, such as Kendo's body in room 101: the player, the enemies and the scripts wait, so `Sce_rnd` stops; 0 in a scene's lines). What's left:

With an ink ribbon (item 0x1E in a slot below the inventory's size, any count) it shows door message 1, the question; without one it puts `fg_stop` back and shows door message 0. No puts `fg_stop` back on the tick after the answer. The messages type with RE 2's timing and cues, and a yes/no's cue goes after the room's sounds in the trace. - **Item definitions.** Weapons, ammo and healing items have their kinds, and the keys are the ids the item table (`0x53DE28`) gives a door key (0x33, 0x43, 0x53 and 0x59 to 0x5E) and the small key; the lighter and the lockpick are special items. Each stack is the table's maximum. The status screen doesn't read the kinds: its USE, CHECK and COMBN follow the executable's tables. Key items work from the door or script that wants them.
- **Save labels.** A save's footer names its player, its count and its place, as Classic REbirth's save list does (`ddraw.dll`'s `0x10080FC0`): the place is string `0x2B` plus the place in CR's `exe/sys.tex` (the 7z archive in `ddraw.dll`), the place being the first data byte of the room's typewriter (`aot_set` of `Sce_Save`), taken from the room the save was made in at conversion time, not from `0x98E9A7` at run time. Room 200's is "Hall". A room with no typewriter keeps its number; no save is made there. `save_200_new` checks the footer's place on the screen (`Leon A/01/Hall`).
- **Item stacks.** Each item holds the item table's maximum (`0x53DE28`): 250 for the handgun, shotgun, magnum and grenade rounds, the ink ribbon and the small key, 240 for the bowgun's, and 100 for the fuel and the SMG and sparkshot ammo. A pick-up of 0x14 to 0x1F joins the first stack of its id with room for all of it, as the window's check does, or takes a free slot; it never splits. The status screen's text stand-in draws the personal slot; its art is S10's.
